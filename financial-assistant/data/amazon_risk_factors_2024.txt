Amazon.com Inc. Risk Factors and Financial Overview FY2024

Financial Summary
Amazon.com reported net sales of $620.1 billion for fiscal year 2024, an increase of 12.3% from $551.8 billion in the prior year. Operating income reached $52.1 billion with an operating margin of 8.4%, a significant improvement from 6.4% in FY2023.

AWS Performance
Amazon Web Services generated $100.3 billion in revenue, growing 18.5% year-over-year and crossing the $100 billion annual revenue milestone. AWS operating income was $35.2 billion with an operating margin of 35.1%. AWS maintained its position as the largest cloud infrastructure provider globally with approximately 31% market share.

North America Segment
North America segment revenue was $387.7 billion, growing 11.8% year-over-year. Operating income improved to $20.1 billion from $14.9 billion, driven by fulfillment network efficiency gains, improved advertising monetization, and increased seller services adoption.

International Segment
International segment revenue was $132.1 billion, growing 8.2% year-over-year on a constant currency basis. The segment returned to profitability with operating income of $1.3 billion, compared to an operating loss of $2.7 billion in FY2023.

Key Risk Factors

Competitive Pressures
Amazon faces intense competition across all business segments. In e-commerce, competitors include Walmart, Shopify merchants, Alibaba, and emerging platforms like Temu and Shein. In cloud computing, AWS competes with Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle Cloud, and increasingly with custom chips from large enterprises. In advertising, Amazon competes with Google, Meta, and a growing number of retail media networks.

Regulatory and Legal Risks
Amazon faces significant regulatory scrutiny globally. The FTC antitrust lawsuit filed in September 2023 remains pending, alleging monopolistic practices in the online marketplace. The EU Digital Markets Act imposes new obligations on Amazon's marketplace platform. India's evolving e-commerce regulations could limit foreign investment in retail operations. Data privacy regulations including GDPR and emerging U.S. state privacy laws create compliance complexity and potential liability.

Labor and Workforce Risks
Amazon employs approximately 1.55 million workers globally. Rising minimum wages, unionization efforts at warehouse facilities, and increased labor market competition present ongoing cost pressures. The company faces multiple NLRB complaints and union organizing campaigns.

Technology and Cybersecurity
As one of the world's largest processors of customer data and financial transactions, Amazon faces persistent cybersecurity threats. A significant data breach could result in regulatory penalties, litigation costs, and reputational damage. The rapid deployment of AI services through AWS also introduces new risks related to AI safety, bias, and regulatory compliance.

Supply Chain and Logistics
Amazon's fulfillment network comprises over 1,500 facilities globally. Supply chain disruptions from geopolitical events, natural disasters, or transportation bottlenecks could impact delivery performance and customer satisfaction. The company's investment in air cargo, delivery vehicles, and last-mile logistics creates significant capital commitments.

Macroeconomic Factors
Consumer spending patterns remain sensitive to inflation, interest rates, and employment conditions. A prolonged economic downturn could reduce discretionary spending on Amazon's platform and slow enterprise cloud adoption. Foreign currency fluctuations impact international segment results.

Capital Expenditure Commitments
Amazon invested $59.3 billion in capital expenditures in FY2024, primarily for AWS infrastructure, fulfillment centers, and transportation. This level of investment creates significant fixed cost commitments and requires sustained revenue growth to maintain acceptable returns on invested capital.
